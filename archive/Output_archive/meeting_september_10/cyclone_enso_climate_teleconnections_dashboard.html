🌀 1. Why 2025 and 2026 Have Zero Landfall Events in the Baseline

Underwriting actuaries inspecting the catalogue often ask why 2025 and 2026 record zero cyclone landfalls across African districts:

  • Consolidated ERA5 vs. Provisional ERA5T: ECMWF ERA5 consolidated quality-controlled hourly reanalysis operates with a 3-month latency. Near-real-time provisional updates are not integrated until finalized by ECMWF.
  • NOAA IBTrACS Post-Season Quality Control: Official IBTrACS best-track archives are finalized 6–18 months after a cyclone season concludes.
  • Physical Suppression (2024–2025 El Niño Transition): The 2023–2024 Super El Niño (+2.03°C) shifted atmospheric convection eastward away from the Mozambique Channel, producing an exceptionally quiet tropical cyclone season with zero major landfalls in Southern Africa.
  • Prospective Portfolio Horizon: 2026 represents the current prospective underwriting policy year; 1980–2024 forms the empirical 45-year historical backtest.

🔬 2. Physical Ocean-Atmosphere Mechanism (Basis Risk Reduction)

Large-scale climate modes dictate seasonal cyclone frequency and severity across the African continent:

  • La Niña Phase (Cool Pacific / Warm SWIO): Weakens upper-tropospheric vertical wind shear over the Mozambique Channel and deepens the monsoon trough. This creates conditions for cyclone clustering—as witnessed in 2000 (Eline & Hudah) and the historic 2020–2023 Triple-Dip La Niña (Eloise, Batsirai, Emnati, Gombe, and Freddy).
  • El Niño Phase (Warm Pacific): Increases vertical wind shear across Madagascar and Mozambique, tearing apart developing tropical disturbances before they reach Cat 1+ strength.
  • Indian Ocean Dipole (+IOD): Creates anomalous warm sea-surface temperatures off East Africa, driving rare northern systems into Somalia, the Gulf of Aden, and Tanzania (e.g., Sagar 2018, Gati 2020, Hidaya 2024).
  • Underwriting Value: Reinsurers can dynamically adjust attachment thresholds and catastrophe loadings based on 6-month seasonal ENSO/IOD forecasts.
