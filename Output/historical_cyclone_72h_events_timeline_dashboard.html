🔬 Agronomic & Engineering Scientific Reference: Wind Lodging & Stem Failure Thresholds

The parametric windspeed tiers calibrated in this study are grounded in biomechanical crop failure physics, plant physiology, and international meteorological damage standards:

🌱 63 km/h (34 kt / 40 mph) — Moderate Root Lodging Floor
WMO Beaufort Force 8 (Gale Force): Dynamic wind pressure ($q = rac{1}{2} ho V^2 pprox 200 ext{ N/m}^2$) generates sufficient bending moment at the stem base to cause root lodging (plants leaning > 30° from vertical), especially when tropical rains have saturated topsoils. Vascular bundles (xylem/phloem) remain structurally intact; plants retain the capacity to "gooseneck" upright. Direct yield loss is moderate (5%–20%), primarily caused by shaded leaf canopies and combine harvest pickup losses.
🌾 90 km/h (49 kt / 56 mph) — Severe Stem Breakage ("Green Snap")
WMO Beaufort Force 10 (Whole Gale/Storm) & NOAA Severe Convective Standard: Wind velocity exceeds 25 m/s, where dynamic drag on the upper tassel/ear canopy surpasses the ultimate flexural bending strength of the lower maize internodes (nodes 3 to 6). Instead of flexible bending, plants suffer irreversible brittle green snap (stalk breakage). Snapping below the ear node halts carbohydrate translocation, causing 100% loss for affected stalks and an irrecoverable 40%–80% field yield collapse.
🌪️ 119 km/h (64 kt / 74 mph) — Catastrophic Crop Flattening & Property Attachment
Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Category 1 Baseline: Dynamic pressure exceeds 700 N/m², resulting in 100% field flattening, stalk uprooting, complete decapitation, and total ear detachment. For agricultural portfolios, this represents total constructive loss (100% payout). For commercial property and infrastructure, this marks the structural attachment floor (roof cladding shear, glazing failure, and industrial uplift).
